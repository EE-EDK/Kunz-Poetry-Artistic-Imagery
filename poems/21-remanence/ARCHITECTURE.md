## 0. Title

**Remanence**

Bare. No article, no modifier, no genitive frame. The word cannot survive a qualifier — anything in front of it switches the reader's parser on and destroys the mechanism.

Three tiers:

1. **Misfiled.** Most readers half-recognize it and read past it as a variant of "remembrance." That misreading is the poem's own mutation rule executed before line one: same shape, meaning shifted from the human to the physical. (Visual and morphological adjacency only — the words are not etymologically related. *Remanence* < Latin *re-* + *manere*, to remain. *Remembrance* < *re-* + *memorari*, to call to mind.)
2. **Definition.** The magnetization remaining in a material after the applied field is removed. The cause is withdrawn; the orientation persists.
3. **Position.** Remanence is the value at zero field on a hysteresis loop — the curve that never retraces its own path. Return that is not return. Carried without being named.

**Why not a memory word.** Memory implies retrieval. Remanence implies residue. Something can remain after its cause has vanished without remaining in its original form. The speaker is not trying to remember; the speaker is discovering that forgetting does not remove what has remained.

**Register rule.** The title arrives cold. Line one arrives domestic. The title sets clinical; the poem undercuts it immediately with furniture.

## 1. Form

Standard sestina, unmodified: 39 lines, six sestets plus a three-line envoi, end-word positions rotating on the fixed **6-1-5-2-4-3** permutation. The mutation is lexical only. Each position is occupied not by a fixed word but by a **family** of six drifting from atmospheric toward specific. Position rotation is rigid; vocabulary is not.

| stanza | L1 | L2 | L3 | L4 | L5 | L6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A | B | C | D | E | F |
| 2 | F | A | E | B | D | C |
| 3 | C | F | D | A | B | E |
| 4 | E | C | B | F | A | D |
| 5 | D | E | A | C | F | B |
| 6 | B | D | F | E | C | A |

Positional opposition (three apart in stanza one) holds for all three pairs: **A/D** door–clock, **B/E** window–room-air, **C/F** breath–mirror.

Every one of the fifteen family pairs falls on consecutive lines exactly twice across the six stanzas. That is a property of the permutation, not of the ordering. "Opposite" can only mean stanza-one positions three apart.

## 2. The six families

| key | family | home object | sequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | crossing | **door** | crossing → doorway → border → limit → edge → threshold |
| B | glint | **window** | glint → gleam → glow → flare → blaze → ash |
| C | breath | **breath on glass** | breath → pulse → stillness → absence → void → hollow |
| D | grief | **stopped clock** | sorrow → mourning → ache → **grief** → loss → regret |
| E | murk | **room-air** | murk → gloom → dusk → shadow → silhouette → shade |
| F | mind | **mirror** | mind → thought → awareness → consciousness → self → being |

A, B, C, E and F advance one step per stanza, strictly monotonic, endpoint in stanza six.

**Family D is the exception.** It peaks early — grief lands in stanza four — then recedes through loss and regret. It is the only family with a past tense. Everything else is still becoming; grief has already arrived, and what remains is smaller than what came.

## 3. Staging — locked

Four objects and one condition. Nothing else furnishes the poem.

| object | role |
|---|---|
| **door** | the event. Glazed — a panel, a sidelight, or a transom. See note below. |
| **window** | wrong light that does not warm |
| **stopped clock** | not broken, simply finished |
| **breath on glass** | the only warm, temporary thing in the room |
| **mirror** | lags one beat |
| **room-air** | not an object; the condition the others sit in |

**The door carries glass. [ADDED v2.0 — resolves a collision.]** The figure's governing physical fact is that it does not fog the glass, and the figure stands beyond the door. Without glass in the door there is no surface for that fact to occur on, and the test would have to migrate to the window — which would move the figure away from the door and break the S5 carrier assignment. A glazed door panel is domestic, ordinary, requires no explanation, and puts the breath test at the boundary where it belongs. Consequence: the room contains four glass surfaces (window, mirror, door panel, and the fogged patch itself) and one stopped mechanism. Do not remark on this.

**Named once.** r3 names the door's glass exactly once, at S5 L2 ("Beyond the door's glass"). It is not remarked on anywhere else.

**The far side gets no furniture.** Whatever lies beyond the door is never described, lit, or given dimension. §10 forbids cosmic imagery; this is the same rule applied to space. The poem has one room. The far side is not a second room — it is the absence of information about a second room.

## 5. Carrier schedule (progressive contamination)

The permutation governs end-words. It has no opinion about which object carries them. Objects are therefore free, and their freedom is scheduled.

**Governing principle: breath is the contaminant.** It is the only anchor that moves, evaporates, and physically reaches other surfaces. Contamination spreads from the one thing in the room that is not fixed.

| phase | stanzas | rule |
|---|---|---|
| Establish | 1–2 | Strict weld. Every family on its home object. The reader must be given the rule before it can be taken away. |
| Contaminate | 3–4 | One swap per stanza. Both swaps dispossess the clock. |
| Dissolve | 5–6 | Free. Zero welds in stanza 5; stanza 6 free except the final line. |
| Reassemble | envoi | Full re-weld — emergent, never stated. See §14. |

**Why both swaps hit the clock.** At the exact moment grief lands, it is not on the clock. It is in the mirror. The object built to hold grief has been dispossessed of it precisely when it arrives.

**What stanza 5 is.** The stanza in which the distinction between object and meaning fails completely. Several assignments should feel almost absurd — the clock carrying *void*, breath carrying *self*, the mirror carrying *blaze* — but none may become ornamental surrealism. Each must remain physically imaginable for one instant. The point is not randomness. The point is that once the fastening system disappears, everything becomes capable of meaning anything. That is what existential freedom feels like at its worst: no stable place for a thing to belong.

**Shape of the whole.** Weld → contaminate → dissolve → one weld survives → total re-weld. The room comes apart and puts itself back together correctly, and what it produces when correctly assembled is absence, an emptied body, and a boundary. A system running to spec and returning nothing.

**Craft consequence — the carrier is the subject. [ADDED v2.0]** With first person kept sparse (§8), the poem needs a grammatical subject for thirty-nine lines. The carrier supplies it. The object acts; the speaker notices. This is the sentence-level form of "ordinary competence losing jurisdiction," and it means the carrier schedule is not only imagery management — it is who is doing things in each line.

## 6. The figure — LOCKED

**Governing fact, single:** the figure looks like a person and fails the smallest physical test by which the room knows a person is there.

**Mechanism:** *absence with an outline*, reinforced by *does not fog the glass*. The other mechanisms — parallax failure, wrong scale, assembly from the room — may occur incidentally but never as independent phenomena. One governing fact only.

It is seen beyond the door. Enough geometry to read as human at a glance — shoulders, head, the vertical interruption of a body — never enough specificity to become an identity.

**Not frightening. Unverifiable.** A real person alters a room: takes up temperature, interrupts light, breathes, shifts weight, casts a changing shadow, makes some minute accommodation necessary. The figure does none of this decisively. It occupies visual space without fully occupying the physical world. That is evidence of presence without the presence — stranger than a ghost, and a continuation of the poem's governing problem rather than a supernatural event.

**Never named as:** dead person · ghost · loved one · visitor · future self · double. Each collapses the uncertainty too quickly. It is not a mystery to be solved. It is an ontological inconvenience.

**How the fact is noticed.** Almost accidentally. A horror mechanism would invite investigation — *I checked whether it breathed*. Here the fact sits inside an ordinary act: the room is cold, the glass should cloud, something is there, and it does not.

**Why it answers the breath family.** Breath has been the poem's only incontrovertible proof that something living is present. The figure is therefore not merely mysterious — it is excluded by the room's own evidence system. The body says *someone is here*. The world says *no*. Both remain true for several lines.

**Why stanza five.** The figure becomes legible exactly where the carrier system enters its free phase. Four stanzas have taught the reader that words have homes. Then silhouette appears on the door: a shape with no secure home, carrying the grammar of a body into an object whose function is crossing. First moment the room seems to contain someone who may not be in the room.

**As realized in r3.** S5 L2 — *a head, shoulders, a silhouette* — seen beyond the door's glass. The test at S5 L5 — *the far side stayed clear, even around a self* — phrased as a presence of clarity, not an absence of fog, so it is the poem's only significant absence and it does not compete with any other negation (r3 contains zero instances of *nothing / no*). S6 L4 keeps the author's line: *Breath touched the glass; beside it lingered a shade.*

**Separation rule. [ADDED v2.0]** The mirror lags one beat and is therefore a proto-double. If it appears next to the figure, the reader fuses them and the figure becomes a reflection — which §6 forbids. In v1.0 the mirror sat at S5 L3, directly beneath the figure at L2. The stanza-5 deck was re-dealt to move the mirror to L6, four lines clear, with zero welds preserved. **The mirror and the figure never occupy adjacent lines.**

## 7. Shade's second sense — LOCKED

**Lean on it. Never explain it.**

*Shade* may keep its sense of dimness while faintly inheriting its older sense of a departed presence. The poem must not signal this as wordplay.

The transition **silhouette → shade** should happen naturally enough that the reader feels, only afterward, that a person has been reduced to a lighting condition. That reduction is central. The figure does not become a ghost. The figure becomes **less ontologically expensive**:

> person-shaped thing → silhouette → shade

Each step requires the reader to grant it less reality.

**The reversal.** The cosmic force comes from the possibility that this is not what happened to the figure. It may be what the room has always been able to perceive. The degradation is in the instrument, not in the object. This is the same claim the title makes: what remains is a measurement, not an event.

---

## 8. The speaker — LOCKED

Human without being biographical.

**Forbidden:** explicit backstory · explanation of who has died · named relationship · childhood memory · stated catastrophe · any declaration that the speaker is grieving.

The human life enters through behavior. The speaker notices where the door is, how cold the room is, that the clock has stopped, whether glass fogs, where a body-shaped absence stands, which side of the threshold they occupy. These are acts of attention. That is enough.

**First person: none. [DECIDED 2026-09-05]** r1 carried three first-person marks; the author removed all three. The person now enters only through the competence idioms — *which is a form of mourning*, *as always*, *which counted as loss*, *of all things* — and through the carrier-as-subject rule in §5. The consequence, accepted: the acts of attention have no named attender, and the voice could belong to the person in the room or to the thing beyond the door. §20 uses that.

**Emotional register.** Not panic. *Ordinary competence losing jurisdiction.* Someone attempting to remain practical while the categories required for practicality quietly fail. The speaker keeps observing correctly. Nothing follows from observation. That is the crisis.

---

## 9. Line one — LOCKED

Domestic exactness. No abstraction, no cosmic imagery, no philosophy, no emotional naming, no gesture toward the title.

The reader must encounter a room before they encounter a poem about existence. The first line's job is one proposition: **something ordinary is wrong, and nobody has yet decided that it is important.**

Candidate room-functions: a door left open · a clock stopped at an inconvenient hour · a window holding the last light · air gone strangely cold. The stopped clock is strongest — it introduces the poem's first broken time without explaining it.

Then the line lands on **crossing**, which must initially appear to belong to the mundane scene. At first it means movement through a doorway. By the end it means the invisible event after which a person can no longer return to the same self. The poem never announces the second meaning.

---

## 10. Scale — LOCKED

**Do not introduce the cosmos.** No stars, no galaxies, no infinite darkness, no universe imagery unless an accidental physical image demands it.

The cosmic dimension is generated entirely by the inadequacy of the room. A room is enough. A person stands inside a few cubic metres of ordinary matter and finds the local rules cannot answer the largest questions: where presence began; what makes a person continuous with who they were; why return does not restore; what remains when the cause of a feeling is gone and the feeling is not; whether consciousness belongs to a body the way a word belongs to its object.

Cosmological in consequence, domestic in evidence. The poem behaves as though the universe has been reduced to one room and is failing a household inspection. **Never say this.**

---

## 11. Human depth — LOCKED

The central emotional mechanism: **recognition without recovery.**

The speaker recognizes the room, the objects, the pattern, the figure as almost human, the words when they return — and recognition never restores what has been lost. This is why remanence beats a memory word: memory implies retrieval; remanence implies residue.

---

## 12. Grief and the figure

**The figure does not represent grief.** That would reduce the poem to allegory.

The formal machine creates a stranger relationship. Grief is a state that arrives after its cause has already left the room. The figure is the corresponding physical problem: a presence whose cause may already have left the room. These rhyme without becoming synonymous.

Grief lands in the mirror in stanza four — the emotional event and the act of self-recognition occupying the same object. Then the door contains the silhouette. Then the breath is asked to authenticate presence. The question shifts from *what happened* to **what counts as evidence that anything happened.** That is where the work becomes existential rather than elegiac.

---

## 13. The surviving weld

**threshold → door**, S6 L6, carries the whole ending. Not hope, not resolution, not transcendence.

A threshold is simply the place where one condition becomes another. The reader has watched the room lose its categories, and the only relationship that survives is a boundary attached to the thing that can actually cross it. The room cannot tell the speaker what is on either side. It can only tell the speaker that there are two sides.

The smallest possible answer. Also the cruelest.

---

## 14. The envoi — LOCKED

**The full re-weld must be completely emergent.** Do not state that the objects have returned to their families. No final explanatory gesture. Do not solve the mechanism.

Write the three lines so the reader realizes retrospectively that the room has quietly resumed its original arrangement — noticing one beat after it happens. That delay is not decoration: the mirror has a one-beat lag built into the architecture from stanza one, and the ending performs the same perceptual trick at structural scale.

**Craft constraint. [ADDED v2.0]** Emergence has a cost the envoi must pay: the objects have to be *present* without being *announced*. If the three lines name all six objects plainly, the reader tallies them and the re-weld reads as a formal flourish. Name obliquely, partially, or by function — enough that the arrangement is true, not enough that it is legible on first pass.

**Required final sensation:** not *everything is back to normal* but **everything is where it belongs, and something is still wrong.** That is remanence.

**As realized in r3.** Objects present, oblique: *the glass* (window), *the minute* (clock), *the reflection* (mirror); only *the door* named plainly, and last. L1 is a single clause; L2 and L3 are semicolon lines — the three no longer share one shape. *Stayed put* carries the final sensation in two domestic words.

---

## 15. Final-word logic

**threshold.** Not a flourish. The poem arriving at the only noun it can still trust.

Three registers, simultaneous and unresolved:

1. a literal architectural feature
2. a human boundary between before and after
3. an epistemic boundary the speaker cannot cross by understanding alone

The poem never chooses between them.

---

## 16. Line craft

The machine is lexical repetition, not rhyme. Nothing requires metre.

1. The end-word sits inside a sentence that earns it.
2. Syntax bends around the word — enjambment, buried placement, clauses running past the landing.
3. The constraint stays invisible. A line should feel like it could end anywhere; the word merely happens to land last.
4. The carrier is named by function, not decoration. Where a carrier is displaced, the dislocation is felt and not explained.
5. The carrier is usually the grammatical subject. The room acts; the speaker notices.

**Standing design tests:**

- Build the six words as a sentence first. If the sentence is false, the rotation is false.
- Each word must need the others. If the poem works without one, cut it.
- Specific must also mean closed — more object, less answer. Shine stays as mysterious as murk.
- The human words make the cosmic feel domestic and the domestic feel cosmic.
- Do not pick words you love. Pick words that force the rest of the poem to exist.
- Lines must earn their end-words.

**Hinge lines:**

| location | event |
|---|---|
| S1 L1 | domestic opening; *crossing* lands as ordinary movement |
| S3 L1 | first contamination; the clock takes *stillness* |
| S4 L6 | peak; *grief* lands, and lands in the mirror |
| S5 L2 | the figure; *silhouette* on the door |
| S6 L4 | *shade* on the breath — the endpoint carried by the thing that disproved it |
| S6 L6 | surviving weld; *threshold* on the door |
| Envoi | emergent re-weld; the poem ends on *threshold* |

## 17. Governing principle — author-facing only

The poem never answers **what is the figure.** It answers, without saying so: *what would it mean to live in a world where presence can no longer be distinguished from its residue?*

The domestic objects are the experiment. The sestina is the apparatus. The recurring words are the hysteresis. Grief is the human case. Consciousness is the unresolved measurement. The threshold is the one boundary that remains after every other correspondence fails. Remanence is the condition of having been changed by a force that is no longer there.

The deepest movement is not *loss → grief → acceptance* but **presence → disturbance → misrecognition → dissolution → rearrangement.**

Nothing is healed. Nothing is explained. The room simply learns to hold the remainder.

**None of the above appears in the poem in any form.** This section is scaffolding. If any of these correspondences becomes visible in a line, the line is wrong.

---

## 18. Collection

Through-line: consciousness waking inside a system that cannot hold it. Endings do not resolve. The mind keeps asking.

## 20. Performance — the voice

**Thesis.** The poem's effect is the gap between what is described and how. A figure that fails the room's breath test is reported in the tone used for the corners going dark. The voice therefore has one job: **be trustworthy enough that its refusal to react is the wrongness.** The listener must believe this voice would say if something were wrong. Then it doesn't.

### Profile

| property | direction |
|---|---|
| Register | Low, chest-resonant, close. Not a whisper — a whisper has no body, and the breath test needs one in the room. The distance of someone reading a paragraph across a small table. |
| Age | Mid-life or older. The competence idioms are dry, and dryness is earned; a young voice reads them as attitude, an older one as habit. |
| Gender | Open. Nothing in the text assigns it. Choose for timbre. |
| Affect | None performed. Not cold — *unexcitable*. Cold is a choice the listener hears; unexcitable is a temperament. Archetypes: a documentary narrator over footage seen many times; a lab notebook read aloud to a colleague. |
| Never | The poet's voice — rising terminal inflection, sing-song lift, meaningful pause before the last word. Each puts a spotlight on the end-words, and the end-words are the machine. Also never: horror (the figure is not frightening), elegy (no tremble), theatre (no projection), ASMR (no breathiness for its own sake). |

### Delivery rules

1. **Stress the subject, swallow the object.** The carrier is the grammatical subject of nearly every line. Put the weight there, early, and let the end-word fall away in a lower, quieter tail. The end-word should sound like the least important word in the sentence. This is the most important direction and it is counter-intuitive — the accumulation happens in the listener, not in the mouth.
2. **Pace ~100 wpm, no variation.** 420 words; roughly four minutes with stanza breaths. Tempo does not change at the peak, the figure, or the end. The clock is stopped; the voice is the breath — steady, with duration, no event.
3. **Dynamics: one level, minus.** No line louder than any other. Only downward variation permitted: the figure lines (S5 L2, L5) and the envoi may sit slightly under. Nothing rises.
4. **Pauses.** Sentence-final falls are natural on de-stressed tails. No added beat before end-words. The one enjambment (S3 L5–6) is read straight through. Stanza breaks get a full audible breath. The envoi gets one beat more silence than the listener expects — the mirror's lag applied to the structure.

### Line-specific

| line | note |
|---|---|

| S2 L5 | *which is a form of mourning* — the small lift of a dry aside. The first place the person shows. |
| S4 L6 | **Do not slow down.** A hair quicker if anything, as if moving past it. Grief arrives before the speaker is ready; the listener should not be ready either. |
| S5 L2 | Lower, not slower. Three things seen, not a list recited. |
| S5 L5 | The one word permitted a small weight: *even*. The semicolon is a real pause — the noticing happens in it. |
| S5 L6 | *of all things* — the lift again, the person again, immediately after the figure. The poem's register in two consecutive lines. |
| S6 L6 | Flat, done. *At its threshold* falls away entirely. |
| Envoi | Lower and slightly slower — the only permitted tempo change, downward. Last word under-landed: the final consonants of *threshold* barely there. The poem doesn't arrive. It stops. |

### The one production risk worth taking

Keep the breaths in — every stanza break, every semicolon, every intake — audible, close, unedited. For four stanzas the listener hears a body in the room, and it becomes the proof of presence the poem says breath is.

From S5 L5 onward, edit the breaths out. The voice continues at the same pace and level, and nothing is breathing. Most listeners notice one beat late, or on the second listen, that the voice failed the same test the figure failed. With no *I* in the text, this leaves open whether the voice belongs to the person in the room or to the thing beyond the door — the exact ambiguity §6 protects. It is the mirror-lag principle executed in the medium, derived from the architecture rather than added to it.

Risk: if the edit is audible as an edit, it reads as a trick. Room tone must be continuous; only the breath goes.

**No music, no ticking, no reverb.** A stopped clock makes no sound and should not be given one. A dry, close room — the walls faintly audible, nothing else.

### Whose voice

The speaker is not a character, which removes the usual argument for a performer. The only question is whether the author's natural reading voice can hold the flatness for four minutes without the poet's lift returning on the end-words. If it can, the author's voice is correct — the competence idioms are his, and a hired reader will make them sound like lines. If not, hire for temperament, not tone: the unexcitable quality cannot be directed into someone who lacks it.
